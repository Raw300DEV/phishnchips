      November 7

      This happened to us.

      Minutes after we made an exchange deposit, two wallets we'd never seen sent our wallet dust.

      Both addresses started with the same 4 characters as the real deposit address. Both ended with the same 4. In a wallet, that's all you see.

      They weren't sending us money. They were planting a fake in our history, waiting for the next time we copied "the usual address" from the last transaction.

      We caught it. Then we built the thing that would have caught it for us.

      Trenchers are the easiest target there is.

      You move SOL all day: bank to burner, burner to exchange, exchange back. Every move lands in your history, and every move tells someone which address to fake.

      • The fake matches both ends.Your wallet shortens addresses to the first and last 4 characters.
      • The bait sits right next to the real one.It arrives a minute or two after your deposit.
      • There's no undo.On Solana, one wrong paste and the SOL is gone. Not pending. Gone.

      The links are the other half: fake claim pages, lookalike wallet sites, "verify in 10 minutes". One signature on the wrong page can empty a wallet.

      House rules.

      Six rules that stop most drains before a tool is even needed.

      1. Copy an address from where it really comes from, never from your transaction history. Check every character, not just the ends.
      2. Nobody "claims" anything by signing a transaction they can't read. Close the tab.
      3. An RSVP never needs your wallet. If a party asks you to connect or sign, it's not a party.
      4. Get links from the official account, not the replies under it.
      5. Use a burner with pocket money for anything you aren't sure about.
      6. "Urgent", "last spots", "verify in 10 minutes": urgency is the scam.

      Questions.

      Is it safe to paste my address here?

      Nothing you paste leaves your browser, because the page blocks all outgoing requests. And your public address is public anyway. Never paste a seed phrase or private key, here or anywhere.

      Does it prove a link or address is safe?

      No, and nothing can. It spots the common tricks. "No common tricks found" means just that. When in doubt, don't click and don't send.

      Do I need to connect my wallet?

      No. There's nothing to connect and nothing to sign. If any site asks you to sign just to "check" something, close it.

      I got a tiny amount from a wallet I don't know. Am I hacked?

      Probably not. That's usually address poisoning: bait in your history, not access to your wallet. Don't interact with it, and don't copy anything from that transaction. Paste your history into the History Scan to see the lookalikes.

      My wallet already warns me sometimes. Why use this?

      Use every warning you can get. This one shows you which characters differ, works on any address from any app, and also checks links.
